Common questions from renters

Which Boston neighborhoods are cheapest to rent in?

Of the areas we publish, Dorchester, Roxbury and Allston consistently sit at the low end for a 1-bedroom, and Back Bay, Beacon Hill and the South End at the high end. The table above is sortable, so you can rank every area by the bedroom size you actually need — the cheapest studio market is not always the cheapest 2-bedroom market.

Is rent in Boston going up or down?

Look at the year-over-year figure at the top of this page: it compares this month against the same month last year for a Greater Boston 1-bedroom. For a single neighborhood, each Rent Index page carries a 13-month history table so you can see whether a specific area is moving with the market or against it.

How much rent can I afford?

A common rule is to keep rent near 30% of gross income — about $2,000 a month on a $80,000 salary, or $2,500 on $100,000. Compare that against the table above to see which areas and bedroom sizes are realistic before you start touring. Boston landlords also commonly ask for first month, last month and a security deposit up front, so budget for the move-in cost as well as the monthly rent.

Is it cheaper to get a roommate?

Almost always, and the gap is bigger than people expect. Splitting a 2-bedroom two ways costs meaningfully less per person than renting a studio alone across Greater Boston — the exact figure is in the takeaways above, and every neighborhood page runs the same comparison for that area.

Why is my neighborhood missing?

We publish a neighborhood and bedroom size only when at least 20 active listings support it that month. Below that, one or two unusual listings can swing the number several hundred dollars, so we show a dash instead of a figure we would not rely on ourselves.

Where does this data come from, and can I republish it?

From Spot Easy's own live listings database — active rental listings posted or verified by licensed real estate agents across Greater Boston, including listings sourced through their brokerages. These are asking rents on real listings, not survey responses, estimates or modeled figures. Everything here is free to republish under CC BY 4.0; see Download the data below.

How Spot Easy Collects This Data

How we collect this data +

Source: All data is pulled from Spot Easy's live listings database — active rental listings posted or verified by licensed real estate agents, including listings sourced through their brokerages.

Geography: Greater Boston — Boston, Brookline, Cambridge, Somerville, Medford, Newton, Everett, Quincy, Watertown, and Malden.

Listings counted: Every listing that was on the market at any point during the reported month, measured from its on-market and off-market timestamps — not a single-day snapshot. Drafts, admin-removed listings, and asking rents below $500 or above $20,000 are excluded. A listing that left the market without a recorded off-market date is excluded rather than estimated, so the count runs conservative.

Deduplication: One listing per unique address + unit + bedroom count, keeping the most recently created record.

Bedroom buckets: Studio / 1 BR / 2 BR / 3 BR / 4+ BR.

Minimum sample size: Cells with fewer than 20 listings are suppressed and shown as —.

Metrics: Average (mean) and median asking rent, 25th and 75th percentile, month-over-month and year-over-year change. Average leads on the published pages; median is carried in the CSV and JSON feed.

  • Month-over-month: same neighborhood × bedroom cell from the previous month's snapshot.
  • Year-over-year: same cell from 12 months prior.
